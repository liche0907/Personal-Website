My research is in international macroeconomics, finance and time-series econometrics, with a focus on exchange rates and asset prices. I use structural-break methods, out-of-sample forecast evaluation, and machine learning on text and market data. My current work examines how central-bank policy regimes, communication, news and other unstructured data shape currency and asset-price responses.

Dissertation: *Essays in International Macroeconomics and Asset Pricing*. Advisor: Marcelle Chauvet. Committee: Dongwon Lee, Colleen O'Brien.

## Working Papers

**Currency-Specific Breaks and Foreign Exchange Puzzles**

Why does the link between interest rates and currencies differ across countries and shift over time, and why does the forward-premium puzzle come and go? I argue that both reflect how strongly central banks use interest rates to defend their exchange rates. Dating regime shifts currency by currency across 45 G10 pairs since 1979, I find that the link between rates and the currency turns to the textbook sign when this defense ends, and never the reverse. Central-bank decision records and daily event studies reveal the mechanism. While defending, banks moved rates in response to the currency, and those moves did little to it. The forward-premium puzzle is concentrated in the same defense regimes and is not explained by global risk. Since 1999, global risk has mattered more than rates for the yen and franc crosses. A segmented-market model in the spirit of Itskhoki and Mukhin (in progress) ties the credibility of the defense to the size of the deviation from uncovered interest parity.

## Work in Progress

**Central-Bank Communication, News Text and Market Reactions**

A text dataset of central-bank communication (2,556 policy-decision documents from nine central banks, 1990–2026; about 13,000 central-bank speeches; 238 FOMC statements) and news (the New York Times archive, 2022–2026, and 9,370 time-stamped 2026 headlines labelled by a chronological LLM after its training cutoff), matched to intraday and daily market data. Preliminary results: currency moves go the “wrong way” two to four times as often (in odds) on Fed and ECB announcements with information effects; banks’ stated exchange-rate motives track their policy regime changes (κ = 0.93); and oil explained 33% of daily five-year breakeven moves in March–October 2026, up from 20% in 2021–23.

**Forecasting Exchange Rates with LSTM Networks and Positioning Sentiment**

LSTM forecasts of five major currencies (Python, TensorFlow/Keras) with a new sentiment index built from futures traders’ positions, weighted for loss aversion. The models do not beat a random walk, which motivated the break-dating approach.

## Earlier Research

**International Trade and Aid Allocation Decisions on a Network Basis** (M.S. Thesis, Tufts University, 2017)

In order to study the self-interest motivation when allocating aid to developing countries, I build a theoretic model based on network theory and input-output economy using a baseline Ricardian international trade setup. The model suggests that disaggregate fluctuations in developing country potentially propagate via international trade and interconnectivity of upstream and downstream industries and cause amplified impact on aggregate productivity in the developed country. I examine the effect of intermediate goods share on aid allocation decision of a donor country facing disasters in the recipient country using a cross-sectional model and a panel model. I found evidence supporting the results of the theoretic model and intermediate goods share of import between donor and recipient countries has a significantly positive impact on aid allocation decision and has a less persistent effect when disasters occur during the past in the recipient country.
